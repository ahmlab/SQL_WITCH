# Writing for SQL Witch

Everything a character says lives in plain-text screenplays under `data/story/`. You can edit
them with any text editor; there is no JSON to escape. The game checks every file when it starts
and, if something is wrong, says which file, which line and what to fix.

| File | What it holds |
|---|---|
| `data/story/intro.script` | The intro (office, void, glade), the guardians' arrivals, the result lines. |
| `data/story/tutorial.script` | Every word of the tutorial. `data/tutorial.json` keeps its structure. |
| `data/story/hints.script` | What the guardians and the Grimoire say when the player is stuck. |
| `data/pacing.json` | How fast text types out, pauses after punctuation, voices, box styles. |
| `data/hints.json` | When the Hint Director lets someone speak, and which hint comes next. |

## The screenplay format

```
# A comment: the game ignores lines that start with #.
== glade_intro ==
[scene glade] [book] [sfx book]
Grimoire: Welcome to Codd, witch. {pause 300} Here the world is a database.
Ada: A... {speed slow}talking{speed normal} book?
<<if next == "tutorial">>
Grimoire: Then show me. {wave}Practice{/wave} first.
<<else>>
Grimoire: Ask the {color gold}slime{/color}.
<<endif>>
<<set met_grimoire = true>>
-> Who are you? => who
-> Let's go. => go
```

- **Nodes.** `== name ==` starts a scene or a conversation. Names use letters, digits and `_`.
- **Lines.** `Speaker: text`. A line with no speaker (`: text`) is narration.
- **Stage directions** in square brackets, before a line (on its own line or at its start):
  `[scene office|void|glade|keep]`, `[sleep 0-3]` (how sleepy Ada is in the office), `[book]`,
  `[slime]`, `[knight]` (who is on stage), `[sfx name]` (a sound from
  `assets/audio/sounds.json`), `[music name]`, `[portrait ada sad]` (a portrait and its
  emotion; the art goes in `assets/portraits/ada_sad.png`), `[shake]` (the screen trembles),
  `[wait 400]` (a silence before the line, in milliseconds). `[end]` ends the conversation.
- **Pacing inside a line.** `{speed fast}`, `{speed slow}`, `{speed normal}`, `{pause 300}`
  (milliseconds) and `{instant}` (the rest of the line appears at once).
- **Effects inside a line.** `{wave}...{/wave}` (letters rise and fall), `{shake}...{/shake}`
  (they tremble), `{color gold}...{/color}` (any palette colour of `engine/render.py`, by name),
  `{rune WHERE}` (a word glowing like a rune).
- **Placeholders.** Any other `{name}` is filled in by the game: `{foe}`, `{count}`,
  `{rows}`, `{mode}`... Plurals follow the ICU format, e.g.
  `{count, plural, one {# spell} other {# spells}}`.
- **Choices.** `-> text => node` lines, one per option, after the line that asks.
- **Conditions.** `<<if ...>>`, `<<elif ...>>`, `<<else>>`, `<<endif>>`. A condition can use
  names (`codex.WHERE` is true once the player has used WHERE), numbers, quoted words or bare
  words (`integrity == damaged`), comparisons (`fatigue > 0.7`), `and`, `or`, `not`, brackets.
  A name the game does not know counts as false.
- **Variables and jumps.** `<<set met_sage = true>>` remembers something; `<<jump node>>` goes
  on in another node.

### Translation keys

Every line gets a stable translation key from its file, node and position:
`story.intro.knight_intro.3` is the third line of the `knight_intro` node of `intro.script`.
Choices are `...choice1`, `...choice2`. The English in the script is the source text the
translators see (prompt 04 extracts it). Adding a line at the end of a node keeps every other key;
inserting one in the middle renumbers the lines after it, so their translations need checking.
Spell and item names stay in English (they are data in the database); only the narration around
them is translated.

### What the player can do while text types

ENTER finishes the line, then moves on. Holding ENTER (or SPACE) types faster; holding CTRL
skips through the conversation. A toggles auto-advance. L opens the log of everything said so
far. ESC skips the whole scene.

## Pacing (`data/pacing.json`)

- `cps` is characters per second: `latin` for spaced scripts, `cjk` for Chinese and Japanese
  (one character carries a word, so it types slower).
- `speakers` makes a speaker slower (0.8) or faster (1.15) than normal.
- `punctuation` adds a pause after a character, in milliseconds. `default` applies to every
  language; a language can add or change its own (`zh-Hans` pauses on `，` and `。`).
- `voices` gives each speaker a blip: a timbre (`square` or `triangle`) and a note from 1 (low)
  to 8 (high), played every `blip_every` letters. New voices are generated by
  `python tools/make_sounds.py`.
- `styles` and `speaker_style` give each kind of speaker its box: narrator, Ada, Grimoire,
  guardian, companion, letter. Colours are palette names from `engine/render.py`.

Long lines are never cut: if a translation is longer than its box, the text scrolls inside the
box as it types.

## Hints (`data/hints.json` and `data/story/hints.script`)

The Hint Director listens to the battle and decides when someone speaks during the player's
turn. It never speaks while the player types, says at most one line per turn, never repeats a
line in a fight, and stays quiet while the player is doing well (`quiet_when`), except for loud
topics such as a damaged world or low HP.

Each topic in `hints.json` has a condition (`when`, in the condition language above) and a ladder
of `steps`, each a node of `hints.script`. `{guardian}` in a step becomes `slime` or `knight`. The
guardian speaks first, in character, with a taunt that hides a real hint; if the player is still
stuck the Grimoire steps in, each step more explicit: an observation, a nudge, the shape of a
query, and finally an offer to show the exact query (F3). To add a hint, write a node in
`hints.script` and add its name to a topic's steps. To make hints come sooner or later, change
`speak_after_idle_s` or a topic's `idle_s`.

A guardian's `{mode}` is its brain's real mode (from `explain()`), in the words of the `hint.mode`
entries of `data/i18n/en.json`, so a taunt never lies about what the guardian is doing.

## The tutorial (`data/story/tutorial.script`)

The tutorial's words are nodes named after the place they fill in `data/tutorial.json`:
`look_intro`, `look_task`, `look_restate`, `practice_book_done`, `coach_miscast`,
`dummy_hit`... In a step, a Grimoire line (or a narration line) starts the step and a
`Training Dummy:` line after it is the dummy's reply in the same step. Keep the number of steps in
a node the same as in `tutorial.json` (the JSON holds what each step points at), or add the new
step there too. The tests measure every line against its box.
